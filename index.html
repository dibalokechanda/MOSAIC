The Key Idea

Overview of the MOSAIC framework. (1) Calibration phase. Patches are randomly sampled from a calibration set of WSIs and encoded by \(K_v\) pathology foundation models, each producing features in its own native dimensionality. Generalized Canonical Correlation Analysis (GCCA) fits projection matrices \(\{\mathbf{W}_k\}\) that map all encoders into a unified \(D\)-dimensional representation space. (2) Global agreement signal. In the unified space, each feature dimension is characterized by how strongly the projected encoder views correlate. The agreement spectrum \(\boldsymbol{\rho} \in [0,1]^D\) separates dimensions where encoders Do Agree (encoder-invariant tissue structure) from dimensions where they Do Not Agree (encoder-specific variation). (3) Local uncertainty signal. At inference time, each patch is projected by all encoders and the cross-encoder variance is computed. Patches with high variance (red Gaussian) indicate encoder disagreement and are treated as locally uncertain; patches with low variance (green Gaussian) indicate consensus and are treated as informative. MOSAIC uses \(\boldsymbol{\rho}\) and \(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^2(x)\) jointly to guide prompt-based MIL aggregation.

Semantic Alignment Calculation: The interpretability of the MOSAIC framework is derived from the alignment between visual patch embeddings and clinical token features. For a given image patch \(i\) with visual feature mean \(\mu_i\) and a clinical text prompt tokenized into tokens \(\{t_j\}_{j=1}^N\), the relevance score \(R_{i,j}\) is defined as the normalized dot product in the joint embedding space:

\[ R_{i,j} = \frac{\mu_i \cdot t_j}{\|\mu_i\| \|t_j\|} \]

The final diagnostic heatmap for a specific clinical term \(j\) is generated by aggregating these relevance scores across the slide manifold, providing a spatially-resolved audit of the model's evidence-based reasoning.

Hierarchical Uncertainty Visualization: The visual interpretability maps are generated through a multi-scale alignment process. For each whole slide image (WSI), feature embeddings are extracted across three heterogeneous backbones (UNI, GigaPath, and ResNet50) and projected into a joint latent space via Generalized Canonical Correlation Analysis (GCCA). The diagnostic uncertainty for each image patch \(i\) is quantified by the variance \(\Sigma^2_i\) across the aligned encoder outputs, representing the degree of consensus among the vision models. These patch-level uncertainty scores are then hierarchically mapped onto the original WSI thumbnail using a high-resolution heatmap overlay.
